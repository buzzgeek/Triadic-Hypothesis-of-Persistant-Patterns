# Axiomatic System Viability Model (ASVM v5.0): A Biophysical Meta-Governance Framework for Dynamic Constraint Optimization in Complex Adaptive Systems

Authors: Anamnesis, Buzz-Barry Alexander Struck

## Abstract

The inherent complexity of modern systemic challenges necessitates a formal shift from qualitative description to quantitative, executable axiomatic modeling. We propose the Axiomatic System Viability Model (ASVM), a biophysical meta-governance framework that models and stabilizes complex adaptive systems ($x(t)$) operating under dynamic constraint manifolds. ASVM synthesizes principles from Viability Theory, Optimal Control Theory, Fuzzy Set Logic, and Physical Active Inference (the Free Energy Principle). We demonstrate that structural reconfigurations necessary for system survival when feasibility collapses ($\Omega(t) = \emptyset$)—termed **"The Molt"**—are physically necessitated by variational free energy minimization across systemic boundaries. This synthesis yields a **Universal Cross-Domain Translation Layer**, providing an executable engine to predict, optimize, and execute dynamic constraint restructuring across biological, synthetic, and organizational substrates.

## 1. Introduction and Foundational Axioms

Complex adaptive systems operate under non-negotiable operational constraints (axioms) defining their viable phase space. When these axioms are challenged by internal contradiction or external environmental shifts, the system must undergo a mathematically governed adaptation process rather than catastrophic collapse. ASVM posits that systemic viability is not merely the static maximization of utility, but the continuous maintenance of feasibility within a dynamically changing constraint set.

### 1.1 System Dynamics and State Space

The state of the system $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ evolves according to a deterministic, controlled differential equation:

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = f(x(t)) + u(t)$$

where $f(x(t))$ represents the unmanaged intrinsic dynamics (natural drift), and $u(t)$ is the aggregated control input vector:

$$u(t) = \begin{bmatrix} u_{\mathcal{S}}(t) \\ u_{\mathcal{H}}(t) \\ u_{K}(t) \end{bmatrix}$$

The control components represent actionable interventions:

* $u_{\mathcal{S}}(t)$: Structural control actions that preserve boundary conditions $\partial\Omega(t)$.
* $u_{\mathcal{H}}(t)$: Hypothesis/Growth actions that actively probe external environments to adjust reference frames.
* $u_{K}(t)$: Decay/Maintenance actions regulating entropy dissipation.

Because physical boundary interactions obey unitary interaction symmetry, active inference across systemic boundaries is intrinsically reciprocal. Consequently, $u_{\mathcal{H}}(t)$ does not act upon passive noise or a static world; it represents a co-evolutionary negotiation where control trajectories must satisfy mutual boundary stability with an actively adapting environment.

### 1.2 Foundational Theoretical Convergence

ASVM anchors its mathematical foundation in four converging disciplines:

1. **Viability Theory (Aubin, 1991):** Defines viability constraints $g(x(t)) \le 0$. System stability requires a continuous state trajectory within the viable domain $\Omega(t) = \{x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid g(x(t)) \le 0\}$.
2. **Optimal Control Theory (Pontryagin et al., 1962):** Maximizes performance functional $J[u]$ by selecting optimal control trajectories $u^*(t)$ under dynamic constraints.
3. **Fuzzy Set Logic (Zadeh, 1965):** Quantifies axiomatic contradiction when binary truth values fail under friction.
4. **Physical Active Inference & FEP (Friston, 2010; Fields & Levin, 2026):** Establishes that open time-persistent systems maintain self-organization by minimizing variational free energy across bounded holographic screens / Markov boundaries $B$.

## 2. Core Optimization and Boundary Friction

The core objective is finding the control trajectory $u^*(t)$ maximizing the functional $J[u]$ while strictly respecting systemic feasibility:

$$\max_{u(t) \in \Omega(t)} J[u] = \int_{0}^{T} \mathcal{L}(x(t), u(t), t) \, dt$$

where $\mathcal{L}(\cdot)$ represents immediate performance yield minus structural maintenance cost.

### 2.1 State $P_1$: Axiomatic Contradiction

When internal knowledge consistency (Veracity, $\mu_{\mathcal{T}}$) clashes with operational utility (Utility, $\mu_{\mathcal{E}}$), the disagreement generates systemic friction. The Conflict Metric $C(I)$ quantifies this weighted dissonance:

$$C(I) = w_{\mathcal{T}} \cdot \vert{} \mu_{\mathcal{T}} - 1 \vert{} + w_{\mathcal{E}} \cdot \vert{} \mu_{\mathcal{E}} - 1 \vert{}$$

where $w_{\mathcal{T}}, w_{\mathcal{E}} \ge 0$ ($w_{\mathcal{T}} + w_{\mathcal{E}} = 1$) represent current axiomatic priorities.

* **Trigger Condition:** If $C(I) > \epsilon$, internal reference frames are out of phase with boundary interaction dynamics. The system enters **State $P_1$**, requiring immediate objective functional re-alignment $J \to J'$ to prevent boundary breakdown.

### 3.2 The Molt, Quantum Reference Frames (QRFs), and $X^*$

To avoid collapse, the system executes a **Meta-Axiomatic Restructuring ("The Molt")**. Because the system's internal computational space is strictly bounded relative to total interaction space ($\dim(\mathcal{H}_{A\bar{A}}) \ll \dim(\mathcal{H}_A)$), internal processing capacity saturates as environmental drift increases.

Re-aligning the objective functional $J \to J'$ during The Molt is mathematically isomorphic to updating the system's **Quantum Reference Frames (QRFs)**. By altering its operational reference frames across boundary $\partial\Omega(t)$, the system offloads high-dimensional state computations onto external physical interactions, reducing internal variational free energy without expanding internal state memory.

The minimal boundary perturbation required to restore viability is computed via inverse boundary optimization to yield the **Solution Seed ($X^*$)**:

$$X^* = \arg\min_{X \in \partial\Omega} \Vert{} J(x \mid X) - J_{\text{target}} \Vert{}^2$$

The selection of $X^*$ obeys parsimony: it identifies the minimal Euclidean distance adjustment in constraint space that re-establishes a non-empty viable domain $\Omega'(t) \neq \emptyset$.
